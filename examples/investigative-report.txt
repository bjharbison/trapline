The Larkmoor Harbor Restoration Fund, a nonprofit created to rebuild the waterfront in the fictional port city of Port Anselm, sent more than four million dollars to companies with no public track record, according to bank records and court filings reviewed for this report.

The fund was founded in 2019 by Teodora Vass, a former city planner. Vass runs the fund from a converted warehouse on the east pier. Board minutes show she appointed her longtime associate, Lucian Brandt, as treasurer in 2021. Brandt had previously worked for Meridale Construction, the firm that later won the largest restoration contract.

Meridale Construction is headquartered in Port Anselm. Its chief executive, Oskar Melnik, met with Teodora Vass at least six times in the months before the contract was awarded, according to calendar entries obtained by this newspaper. Melnik declined to comment.

The records show that Meridale Construction subcontracted most of the work to Greyfen Supply, a company registered in Valletta two weeks before the contract was signed. Greyfen Supply has no website and no listed employees. Its sole director is Mira Castellane, who is also the sister-in-law of Lucian Brandt.

Greyfen Supply in turn paid Talmont Advisory, a consulting firm based in Luxembourg, for what invoices describe as "logistics planning." Talmont Advisory is managed by Rafael Duchene, a financial adviser who previously partnered with Lucian Brandt on a property venture in Lisbon.

Hendrik Aalto, an accountant who joined the fund in 2020, first raised concerns internally. He resigned from the fund in March after, he said, his questions went unanswered. Aalto later provided copies of the invoices to the Port Anselm Ethics Commission.

The Ethics Commission accused Teodora Vass of failing to disclose her ties to Meridale Construction. In a written response, Vass said every contract followed the fund's procurement rules and that she had no financial interest in any supplier.

Investigators at the commission are now examining whether money paid to Greyfen Supply returned to anyone at the fund. Commission director Nadia Ferreol said the inquiry would take several months. "We follow the invoices wherever they go," she said.

Meridale Construction also acquired Pellar Marine Works, a smaller dock repair company, shortly after the contract was awarded. Pellar Marine Works had been owned by Rafael Duchene until the sale.

Residents of the east pier say they have seen little progress. Ines Rourke, who leads the East Pier Tenants Association, said the promised seawall is still unfinished. Rourke met with commission investigators in April and gave them photographs of the site.

Lucian Brandt did not respond to repeated requests for comment. Mira Castellane could not be reached.

The Larkmoor Harbor Restoration Fund said in a statement that it welcomed the review and would cooperate fully with the Ethics Commission.
